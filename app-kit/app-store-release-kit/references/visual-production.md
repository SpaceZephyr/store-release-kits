# App Store visual production

## Icon

- Develop three original directions before committing to one.
- Use a clear silhouette, distinctive color logic, and one dominant idea.
- Keep critical detail away from edges and test at small rendered sizes.
- Supply a full square master; let the system apply platform masking.
- Avoid tiny text, UI screenshots, Apple hardware silhouettes as decoration, trademark lookalikes, or unlicensed symbols.
- Plan default, dark, clear, and tinted behavior when the target toolchain supports them.
- Integrate the approved icon into Icon Composer or the actual target asset catalog; a loose PNG in release-assets is not enough.

## Screenshots

Build a narrative:

1. immediate value
2. primary action
3. visible result
4. differentiator
5. trust, collaboration, customization, or completion

Use real UI and synthetic data. Keep copy short, large, localized, and inside safe margins. Do not show personal notifications, real accounts, phone numbers, addresses, tokens, health records, financial records, or customer content.

Use one device family and orientation per set. Never stretch or crop one device's UI into another specification. Preview the first three frames together because they dominate the product page.

## App Preview

Use captured product interaction as the base. Create a 15–30 second story:

- hook and context
- primary interaction
- result
- optional secondary feature
- brand close

Avoid cinematic footage unrelated to the app. Keep overlays honest and readable without relying on audio.

## Rights and accessibility

- Track the source and license for fonts, photos, illustrations, mockups, icons, and audio.
- Do not place Apple trademarks or another app's icon/name in metadata without permission.
- Maintain contrast, avoid color-only meaning, and include representative accessibility behavior when claimed.
- Keep editable source files in `release-assets/source/` and exported upload files elsewhere.
